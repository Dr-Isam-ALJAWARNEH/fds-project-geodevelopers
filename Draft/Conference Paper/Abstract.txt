Air pollution monitoring is crucial for human health. Although it is of highlighted importance, the deployment of a dense sensor network requires high costs, and is challenging to install and maintain, as the real-world pollution datasets are often large and complex. This paper provides a framework for exploring and analyzing geo-referenced air quality data using clustering methods (e.g., K-means) and the Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) algorithm. In addition, it demonstrates of how to simplify dense environmental data and find optimal sensor placements to derive valuable trends and insights. This study showcases how MST structuring, with effective clustering, can improve the analysis and efficiency of pollution monitoring networks. The paper provides an accessible approach for researchers working with complex environmental data.
